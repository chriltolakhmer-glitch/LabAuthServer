## Evaluation

Evaluation means non-production activity for reviewing or testing the product without using it to provide live organizational or customer services.

Subject to vendor approval and the applicable written terms, evaluation may include:

- source inspection
- architecture and security review
- internal demonstration
- internal proof of concept
- isolated development or test evaluation
- non-production validation

Evaluation does not generally authorize:

- production or live use
- customer-facing production deployment
- commercial service delivery
- redistribution
- resale
- sublicensing
- derivative commercial product use
- integration into a competing product
- removal or bypass of licensing controls or notices
- general source modification rights

Any narrow source-modification permission requires separate written authorization.

## Production

Production means any live operational, customer-facing, business-service, or business-critical use involving real users, systems, or workflows. Production use requires vendor authorization, an applicable commercial agreement, and a vendor-issued signed technical entitlement.

A valid technical license does not replace commercial permission, and evaluation access does not create production rights.

## Technical State vs Business Permission

Missing, invalid, unsupported, or expired license content produces restricted Community behavior as a technical runtime state. That state is not a legal evaluation grant and does not authorize production or commercial use. Evaluation or production permission comes only from the applicable vendor-approved written terms and commercial authorization.

## Development, QA, Staging, and Pilots

These labels do not automatically determine whether an environment is evaluation or production. Classification depends on whether the environment supports real operational activity, real users, customer-facing services, or business workflows.

- Development or QA may be evaluation when isolated and non-production.
- Staging may be evaluation when it does not support live users or real operations.
- A pilot requires explicit classification; a time-bounded non-live proof of concept may be evaluation, while a pilot serving real users or business operations is production in substance.
- Where classification is uncertain, obtain vendor approval before use.

## Technical Boundary

A party controlling the source code, build process, and host can technically modify an unofficial build and bypass client-side licensing enforcement. Technical licensing is an authenticity and entitlement control, not unbreakable DRM.
